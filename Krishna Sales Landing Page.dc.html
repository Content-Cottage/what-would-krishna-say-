THE BATTLEFIELD HAS CHANGED.
BUT THE FEELING HAS NOT.

Around five thousand years ago, a warrior named Arjuna stood frozen on a battlefield, unable to move. Every option in front of him had a real cost, and none of them felt clean. That conversation — between Arjuna and his charioteer, Krishna — is preserved in the eighteen chapters of the Bhagavad Gita.

The battlefield looks different today. It might be a career decision you can't stop second-guessing. A relationship that no longer makes sense. A family expectation that feels impossible to meet. A comparison spiral that starts the moment you open Instagram. Or the quiet, exhausting feeling of not knowing what you're supposed to do with your life — while everyone else seems to have already figured it out.

If any of that sounds familiar, this book was written with you in mind.

AN ANCIENT CONVERSATION FOR A VERY MODERN PROBLEM.

This book is not a traditional commentary on the Bhagavad Gita. It does not attempt to replace the work of scholars, spiritual teachers, or the many translations that already exist.

What it attempts is more modest and more personal: to ask what some of Krishna's core teachings might say to a person living in the modern world, facing the specific pressures, fears and confusions of contemporary life. The language is plain. The tone is direct. The intention is to be genuinely useful, not merely inspiring.
